# "What would happen if you got lost alone in the Amazon rainforest?" — 3D animated Short

Vertical 9:16 (1080×1920), 30 fps, ~122 s. Made in **Blender 4.5.15 LTS** (Cycles), fully scripted and
reproducible, with hand-keyed character performance layered over a CC0 motion library.

* Final video: `output/What_If_Lost_In_The_Amazon.mp4`
* Blender project: `blend/AmazonShort_Project.blend` (master file: every shot scene linked from `blend/shots/Sxx.blend`
  plus an `EDIT` scene with the cut, VO and music on the VSE timeline), character `blend/char_explorer.blend`,
  boat driver `blend/char_boatwoman.blend`, plant library `blend/plants_lib.blend`, motion library `blend/anim_lib.blend`.
* Everything is generated by `scripts/` — see "Rebuild" below.

## Story & narration (fact-checked)

Second-person narration ("you"), told chronologically over five days; the explorer visibly deteriorates
(sweat → mud and grime → insect bites → infected cut → gaunt, stumbling) through a per-object `condition` / `sweat`
property that drives his materials.

| # | Line | Notes / sources |
|---|------|-----------------|
| L01 | What would happen if you got lost alone in the Amazon rainforest? | Hook |
| L02 | It starts with a few steps off the trail. You turn around... and the trail is gone. | Most wilderness "lost" cases start with a short detour off a trail. |
| L03 | Every direction looks the same. The canopy hides the sun, and your phone has no signal. | Closed canopy blocks most direct sunlight; no cell coverage in most of the Amazon basin. |
| L04 | Experts say that when you're lost, you should stop and stay put. But that only works if someone knows where you are. And nobody does. | S.T.O.P. (Stop-Think-Observe-Plan) and "stay put" are standard SAR advice (e.g. US NPS / Forest Service "Hug-a-Tree"); staying put works when someone knows your route — hence the closing tip. |
| L05 | So you walk. Without the sun or landmarks, people often walk in circles without realizing it. | Souman et al., *Current Biology* 2009, "Walking straight into circles" — people walking without sun/landmarks repeatedly walked in circles. |
| L06 | It's over thirty degrees, and the air is so humid your sweat barely evaporates. You're soaking wet, but your body can't cool down. | Amazon lowland daytime temps ~30–35 °C, relative humidity commonly 80–100 %; evaporative cooling falls as humidity rises. |
| L07 | You find a stream. But untreated, this water can carry parasites and bacteria, and diarrhea would drain your body even faster. | Untreated surface water can carry *Giardia*, *Cryptosporidium* and pathogenic bacteria (CDC). Diarrhoea accelerates dehydration. Wording avoids the myth that it is "always deadly" — it is a last resort, not forbidden. |
| L08 | Then the afternoon rain arrives. Rainwater is one of the safest drinks out here, so you catch every drop you can. | Afternoon convective downpours are typical; rain collected directly (not from the ground) is far safer than standing/river water. |
| L09 | And the stream gives you a plan. Small streams flow into bigger rivers, and rivers are where people live. So you follow it downstream. | Classic jungle survival guidance (e.g. US Army FM 3-05.70 Survival): follow water downstream to settlements; Amazon communities and boat traffic are on rivers. |
| L10 | Night falls fast. Under the canopy, it's pitch black, and the forest only gets louder. Soaked to the skin, you shiver, even in the tropics. | Near the equator dusk is short; night temps drop to ~20–22 °C and being wet causes chilling/shivering. |
| L11 | By day three, insect bites cover your skin, and a small cut on your arm is red and swollen. In this heat, wounds can get infected in days. | Warm, humid conditions favour rapid skin/wound infection. |
| L12 | Then you nearly step on a fer-de-lance, the snake behind most bites in the Amazon. You freeze, back away slowly, and give it space. | *Bothrops atrox* (common lancehead) causes the majority of snakebites in the Amazon region. Correct response: stop, back away, give it space (no cutting/sucking myths shown). |
| L13 | You're starving, but unknown berries and mushrooms could poison you. You can last weeks without food. Water is what keeps you alive. | Healthy adults survive weeks without food but only days without water; unknown plants/fungi are a poisoning risk. |
| L14 | On day five, the stream opens onto a wide river. And then you hear it... an engine. | Long-tail "peque-peque" canoes are the Amazon's road traffic. |
| L15 | In 1971, after a plane crash, seventeen-year-old Juliane Koepcke survived eleven days alone in the Amazon, by doing exactly this. Following the water. | LANSA Flight 508, 24 Dec 1971, Peru. Koepcke (17) followed a creek downstream, reached a loggers' hut and was found on day 11. |

Myths deliberately **not** used: drinking from any vine, piranha attacks, sucking snake venom, tourniquets, "jaguars hunt people",
quicksand. Closing card: *tell someone your route and when you'll be back.*

## Production pipeline

| Stage | Script | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Narration | `narration.json`, `tts.py`, `asr_check.py` | Kokoro-82M TTS (voice `af_heart`), Whisper (faster-whisper small.en) for pronunciation check + word timings |
| Timeline | `timeline.py` | VO placement, cut points on word timestamps |
| Character | `char_build.py` | Quaternius Universal Base Character head + Modular Outfit parts bound to one rig; rebuilt materials with `condition`/`sweat` driven sweat stains, mud rising from the boots, grime, insect bites, skin sheen; daypack + bottle |
| Motion | `anim_lib_build.py`, `shotlib.py` | Universal Animation Library 1+2 clips on NLA tracks, stride-matched to path speed, terrain-following root, plus hand-keyed COMBINE layers: head/eye-line looks, breathing, shivering, brow wipe, swat, phone raise, waving, reach-and-refuse, flinch, backing away |
| Plants | `leaf_atlas.py`, `plants_build.py` | Philodendron, understory palm, heliconia, shrub and leaf-litter assets built from scanned ambientCG leaves (opacity/normal/roughness, translucency) |
| Environment | `env.py` | Terrain with mud/leaf-litter/puddle blend, stream channel or river basin; buttressed kapok-like giants, pole trees, palm-crowned understory, lianas, canopy masses; GN scatter of Poly Haven scans (ferns, calathea, anthurium, nettle, roots, bark debris, branches) with per-instance wind; flowing water shaders |
| Aerial | `aerial.py` | Canopy of individual multi-lobed crowns (rare flowering ipê/purple crowns), meandering river, tributary, stilt huts, drifting mist |
| Creatures / props | `creatures.py` | Blue morpho, mosquitoes, fireflies, fer-de-lance (taper body, lance head, heat-pit stripe, flicking tongue), Giardia & bacteria, phone, berries, dugout canoe with long-tail motor |
| FX | `fx.py` | GN rain streaks, wet leaves, stronger wind, lightning, night light rig |
| Shots | `shots/Sxx.py` | One script per shot (+ shared sets `rest_set`, `stream_set`, `snake_set`, `river_set`) |
| Render | `render_queue.sh` | Background Cycles render queue (CPU, 4 spp + OIDN, persistent data, camera culling, motion blur on character/camera) |
| Sound | `audio_mix.py` | VO + three music cues with ducking + ambiences + foley/SFX, loudness-normalised to −14 LUFS |
| Captions / cards | `captions.py`, `overlays.py` | Word-synced ASS captions (key words gold), info cards |
| Edit | `assemble.py`, `master_blend.py` | Upscale, grain, overlays, captions, mux; master .blend with linked scenes + VSE edit |

### Rebuild
```
blender -b --factory-startup -P scripts/char_build.py
blender -b --factory-startup -P scripts/char_build.py -- boatwoman
blender -b --factory-startup -P scripts/anim_lib_build.py
blender -b --factory-startup -P scripts/plants_build.py
for s in scripts/shots/S??.py; do blender -b -P $s; done      # writes blend/shots/Sxx.blend
scripts/render_queue.sh                                      # renders shots listed in renders/queue.txt
python3 scripts/audio_mix.py && python3 scripts/captions.py && python3 scripts/overlays.py && python3 scripts/assemble.py
```

## Known limitations (honest notes)

* Rendered on a 4-core CPU with no GPU: Cycles at 46–50 % resolution (≈500×890), 4–6 samples + OpenImageDenoise, then
  Lanczos-upscaled to 1080×1920 with light sharpening and film grain. Fine detail is softer than a native 1080p render.
* No facial rig on the CC0 character: emotion is carried by head/eye-line, posture, breathing and timing, not facial expressions.
* The voice is synthetic (Kokoro TTS), not a human narrator.
* Aerial canopy is a procedural crown surface (no suitable CC0 3D rainforest canopy trees were available), so aerials read
  more stylised than the ground-level shots.
* Some sound effects come from vintage library collections marked CC0 by their Internet Archive uploaders (flagged in CREDITS.md).
