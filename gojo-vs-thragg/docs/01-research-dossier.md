Compiled from five parallel research lanes. Every crossover-critical, medium- or low-confidence, or citation-heavy claim was then **independently re-checked by an adversarial fact-checker** who tried to refute it with fresh searches. The verdict column shows the result; where a claim was corrected, the corrected wording is what appears here. Refuted claims are struck through and are NOT used by the production.

**Source policy.** Citations are to JJK manga chapters / anime episodes and Invincible comic issues / animated episodes. Several fan wikis were unreachable (HTTP 402) and were read via their API or search excerpts; nothing here was read directly off the printed page, so medium/low-confidence items are treated with care in `02-crossover-rules.md`.

**Continuity differences**

- Everything from Gojo's release from the Prison Realm through his death (manga ch 221-236) is manga-only. The anime ended Season 3 (The Culling Game Part 1, eps 48-59) around ch 179, and Part 2 had no premiere date in the sources found (latest about July 2026). That puts the 200% Hollow Purple, all five Unlimited Void clashes, repairing burnout with RCT, his on-page Black Flashes, Mahoraga's adaptation to Infinity and the World-Cutting Slash outside anime canon.
- Recovery from the 0.2-second Unlimited Void (ch 89 / anime ep 33): secondary reviews of S2E9 report narration saying the B5F civilians returned to normal life two months later. In the manga, ch 223 has Ijichi say the survivors were rehabilitated with no lasting effects; I could not confirm a 'two months' figure in the manga text.
- Shibuya staging (anime ep 32 / S2E8 vs manga ch 83-84): according to a Japanese episode review (VG+), the anime moved Gojo's entry point (B1F of Shibuya Hikarie in the manga, B3F in the anime), reordered scenes, and cut one gory civilian death. No change to how Infinity, Domain Amplification or Blue work was reported.
- No differences in technique mechanics between manga and anime were found for the adapted Gojo material: Infinity vs Jogo (ep 7), Kyoto Hollow Purple (ep 20), both Toji fights (eps 27-28), automatic Infinity (ep 29), Shibuya (eps 32-33). The anime mainly adds visual flourish, such as Red drawn as a large fingertip blast and Unlimited Void as a galaxy-filled void.
- Author supplementary material is a separate tier from the manga story: the Official Fanbook (blindfold, Six Eyes vision while covered, Domain Amplification definition), volume extras (Vol. 2 pp107, 146, 166, 188) and the Jump GIGA 2022 Q&A (taijutsu equal to Kenjaku). It was read through the fandom wiki and fan translations, not the original printings.

**Open questions → resolved as explicit creative rulings in 02-crossover-rules.md**

- Would Unlimited Void's guaranteed hit even register a being with no cursed energy (Thragg)? In canon, domains fail to recognize zero-cursed-energy humans (Maki, ch 198), but Unlimited Void is never tested against such a target, and whether a non-JJK alien counts as having zero cursed energy is a crossover ruling, not canon.

- Is there any upper limit on the speed or mass Infinity can stop? Canon gives none and never shows Infinity broken by brute force. The automatic filter weighs speed and mass as danger signals, but nothing says it can be overwhelmed.

**Open questions → resolved as explicit creative rulings in 02-crossover-rules.md**

- Neither canon says whether Thragg or Viltrumites have cursed energy. In JJK, cursed energy comes from negative emotion in humans and in Modulo's Simurians, and zero cursed energy is reached only through the physically gifted Heavenly Restriction. The production must choose one rule and state it as a crossover premise. The choice decides whether closed domain barriers and sure-hits recognise Thragg (G2-25, G2-26), how well the Six Eyes can read him (G2-29, G2-30), and whether Unlimited Void can affect him (G2-22, G2-23).
- No canonical upper limit on the mass, force or speed active Infinity can stop exists, and no scene shows raw force getting through. Any storyboard beat where Thragg's strength alone pushes through Infinity would be invented, not canon.
- If Thragg is ruled to have zero cursed energy like Toji or Maki, Infinity's automatic filter still weighs mass, speed and shape (ch 76), so it would presumably still stop him. That is inference, not a shown scene. A blindfolded Gojo might perceive him only indirectly (Fanbook p 43). Need to decide whether Gojo fights uncovered.
- Malevolent Shrine (an open domain whose Dismantle targets things without cursed energy) is the only shown domain built to hit zero-CE targets. Unlimited Void is a closed-barrier domain. Whether Unlimited Void could hold a being with no cursed energy is untested in canon. The ch 198 rule suggests it could not, unless the caster uses a physical structure as the barrier or the target consents.
- Need the official English wording of ch 198 pp 10-11 (the 'treated like a building' passage) and ch 258 pp 12-13 (whether Sukuna kept the open barrier to catch Maki). Both are cited here through the fan wiki and fan-site summaries.
- Do animals in JJK have cursed energy? No canonical statement was found.
- Official Fanbook p 43 (Six Eyes and blindfold) and p 158 (Infinity) are cited through fan translations. Verify against the physical databook if exact wording matters for production notes.

**Continuity differences**

- Invincible animated (Prime Video) Season 4, which premiered March 18, 2026 with an 8-episode finale on about April 22, 2026, adapts the Viltrumite War. In the show, Viltrum is destroyed when Space Racer's beam (called the 'Infinity Ray' in the Looper recap) passes through it with Nolan, Mark and Thaedus flying behind it, and Thragg decapitates Thaedus. In the comics the same events happen in Invincible #75 (Space Racer's gun destabilizes the core, then Thragg decapitates Thaedus).
- In Season 4 Episode 8 of the show, Thragg meets Mark in Earth's atmosphere and proposes that the surviving Viltrumites blend in with humans and leave Earth alone. In the comics the equivalent truce is in Invincible #76-78, with 37 survivors.
- As of 2026-10-13 the animated series has not adapted Thragg's exile (#102), the Thraxa breeding programme or the Battle Beast duel (#115-120), Oliver's killing (#132), Nolan's death (#138/#141), or Thragg's death in the Sun (#140). In the show Thragg is alive at the end of Season 4, and Season 5 is officially slated for 2027.
- Lee Pace voices Thragg in the animated series. The comics character has no voice reference.
- TV Tropes (secondary and subjective) says the show presents Thragg as even stronger than in the comics. Do not import animated-only feats into a comics-continuity profile.
- Secondary sources disagree on Thragg's comics costume colours (crimson in place of white versus all white). Animated designs may differ again, so pick one continuity's palette on purpose.
- Looper's Season 4 recap says the show adds a line to Thaedus's Scourge Virus warning that is not in comic #78. This is minor, but it is another sign that the show and the comics diverge in dialogue.

**Continuity differences**

- Debut timing: Thragg first appears in the animated series in Season 4 Episode 2 (2026), seasons later in the story than his comics introduction.
- The S4E7 flashback (Argall's court, Thaedus assassinating Argall, Thragg ordering Viltrumites to kill each other) is reported by ComicBook.com as material never shown in the comics.
- The punch whose wind-up pulls opponents in (described as gravity, vacuum or whirlpool) is reported as show-original; Bam Smack Pow says comics Thragg never did this.
- Finale parley: in the comics, Mark and Nolan meet Thragg on Earth together. In the show (S4E8), Mark meets him alone (The Ringer).
- Battle Beast is alive after S4E7 in the show; Thragg only tosses him aside. In the comics, Thragg kills Battle Beast after a long duel (around #115-#120) and wears his fur. So the animated fur shawl is not Battle Beast's pelt.
- Costume: Pace describes the animated uniform as red and white with a cape, Grecian skirt and fur shawl. Fan-wiki descriptions of the comics uniform give crimson and gray with no cape mentioned. Unconfirmed; verify against panels and stills.
- Art style: the animated Thragg follows the show's Cory Walker-influenced style, versus Ryan Ottley's more detailed and menacing comics rendering.
- Season 4 compresses Mark and Nolan's time away from Earth compared with the comics, where they are gone for nearly a year (Looper).
- Tech Jacket in the show is Zoe Thompson, not the comics' Zach. This is peripheral to Thragg but appears in his S4E7 battle.

**Continuity differences**

- JJK release status as of 2026-10-13: the anime has adapted the manga only up to ch.181. Season 3 'The Culling Game Part 1' ran from January 8 to March 27, 2026 (JP), ending with ep.59 'Sendai Colony'. Culling Game Part 2 / Season 4 is announced but undated. Everything from Gojo vs Sukuna (ch.221-236) is therefore manga-only: amplification against Infinity in that fight, the barrier flip and shrink, RCT brain repair, the 200% and Unlimited Purples, Mahoraga's adaptation and the world-cutting slash. Maki's immunity to domains (ch.198) is also manga-only.
- JJK material that is animated with the same content as the manga: Infinity's mechanism and the Jogo fight (S1E7), the Hanami Purple (S1E20-21), Toji and the Inverted Spear and Purple (S2E3-S2E4), automatic Infinity (S2E5), Shibuya amplification, the Hanami crush and the 0.2 s Unlimited Void (S2E8-S2E9), and the burnout rule (ep.45; S3E12).
- Thragg vs Mark's punches: in the comics Mark draws a nosebleed at Viltrum (#75); in the animated S4E7 and S4E8 Mark cannot visibly hurt Thragg at all. A Prime Video fan-wiki note also says Mark makes Thragg bleed on Earth in the comics, but the Image Comics wiki's #77 summary says Mark's attack had no effect. Treat the Earth-bleed claim as unverified.
- Thragg's fate: in the comics he dies in the Sun in #140; in the animated series he is alive after S4 (finale April 22, 2026), with Season 5 expected in 2027.
- Thragg's backstory: the animated S4E7 makes him Regent under Argall and Argall's protege, and shows him ordering the Great Purge. In the comics he fought in the civil war after Argall's death and was then made Grand Regent (#102). This is per the Prime Video fan wiki's trivia.
- Destruction of Viltrum: both continuities use Space Racer's weapon plus Thaedus, Nolan and Mark flying through the planet (comics #75; animated S4E7). The claim that Thragg was uninjured by the shock wave is sourced only for the comics.
- The truce meeting: in the comics Thragg confronts Mark and Nolan over Earth (#77). In the animated S4E8 he meets Mark alone in the upper atmosphere, and Mark is more submissive.

**Added by the fact-checker (facts the lane missed)**

- Infinity can be deactivated only by Gojo himself, or dismissed by a domain (Domain Expansion sure-hit or Domain Amplification). Cursed tools with technique-cancelling effects can also disturb or dispel it. Source: JJK manga ch.84 pp.12-13 (anime ep.32); JJK0 Vol.0 p.165; ch.71 p.19 (anime ep.27). Located via jujutsu-kaisen.fandom Limitless (secondary). This decides that a being with no jujutsu has no in-canon way to switch Infinity off.
- A domain's sure-hit can sometimes be resisted with cursed-energy reinforcement, but this is impractical. The real defenses are anti-domain techniques such as Simple Domain and Hollow Wicker Basket. Source: JJK manga ch.171 p.5 (anime ep.58), via Domain_Expansion (secondary). A combatant with no cursed energy can use none of these.
- Barriers closed off by using the shape of the surrounding environment as their shell carry no sure-hit effect. So the 'use a physical structure as the shell' trick for trapping a zero-cursed-energy target costs the domain its sure-hit. Source: JJK manga ch.171 p.6 (anime ep.58), via Domain_Expansion (secondary).
- Unlimited Void and Hollow Purple need the Six Eyes fully uncovered, so Gojo removes his blindfold first. Domain Expansion also needs the user's specific hand sign. Source: JJK manga ch.15 pp.8, 13-15 (anime ep.7); ch.52 pp.14-17 (ep.20), via Six_Eyes and Domain_Expansion (secondary). These are required storyboard beats.
- The Six Eyes see like a high-definition infrared camera, work through a blindfold, and pick out figures several kilometers away. They do not need cursed energy to function. Source: JJK manga ch.52 pp.10-11, 14 (ep.20); ch.70 p.9 (ep.27); ch.90 p.15 (ep.33), via Six_Eyes (secondary). **Open questions → resolved as explicit creative rulings in 02-crossover-rules.md**

- Does Thragg have cursed energy? JJK canon ties cursed energy to (human) negative emotions and never addresses aliens. The answer decides whether Unlimited Void's sure-hit can target him at all (Maki precedent, ch.198), whether a domain barrier can hold him or he can walk through it like Maki, and how well the Six Eyes track him (Toji precedent, ch.71). The storyboard needs an explicit crossover ruling.
- If Unlimited Void does reach Thragg, does information overload work on a Viltrumite brain? Canon shows it on humans, cursed spirits and Sukuna. There is no Invincible precedent for psychic or information attacks on Viltrumites in either continuity.
- Infinity vs a Viltrumite strike: JJK canon has no force threshold, and every breach in canon came from jujutsu, a tool or adaptation. The storyboard should not show Thragg 'punching through' Infinity by strength alone without flagging it as non-canon invention.
- Air, pressure and sound through Infinity: no explicit statement exists. Visibility and conversation clearly pass; smoke can blind Gojo (ch.233). A storyboard ruling is needed for shock waves and air displacement from a Viltrumite at speed.
- Burnout: canon explicitly says only that teleport is unavailable while Limitless is burnt out (ch.226). That Infinity is also down is a strong inference, not an on-page statement.
- Hollow Purple vs Viltrumite durability: the only canonical defense is cursed-energy reinforcement (Sukuna). Whether a body with no cursed energy but extreme physical density is erased (like Toji's torso) or survives is not decidable from canon.
- Would erased flesh regrow? No Viltrumite in canon regrows a limb, and large injuries take days to months to heal. Precise healing rates for Thragg himself are never given in panels or on screen; the fan wiki's 'minutes to days' is not canon.
- Viltrumite reaction speed relative to Gojo's Blue dash and teleport: neither franchise gives numbers. The handbook's faster-than-light figure is travel in space via subspace jumps, not combat perception.
- Whether Gojo could use Unlimited Void on Thragg while Thragg is grabbing him: the touch exemption (ch.15, ch.227) means a grappling Viltrumite would be immune by JJK rules.
