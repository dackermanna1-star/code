# Tsutomu Yamaguchi (山口彊, 1916–2010): verified fact sheet

Compiled 2026-11-04. Labels:
- **CONFIRMED**: two or more independent reliable sources, or one primary/authoritative source.
- **UNCERTAIN**: the sources conflict, or there is only one weak or secondary source.

## Basic biography

- **CONFIRMED:** Mitsubishi Heavy Industries ship-design engineer at the Nagasaki Shipyard (造船設計技師), on a roughly three-month assignment to Mitsubishi's Hiroshima Shipyard. [DOC] says he went in May 1945. Sources: [DOC], [WEEK] (Telegraph: "three-month assignment for the shipbuilding division"), [HIST] (oil-tanker design), [AP2010].
- **CONFIRMED:** He was 29 in August 1945. Sources: [NYT2010], [DOC], [HIST].
- **Birth date 16 March 1916: UNCERTAIN.** Only English and Japanese Wikipedia give it; I found no primary source. The ages in the obituaries (29 in 1945, 93 at death) are consistent with it.
- **CONFIRMED:** Two colleagues from Nagasaki were with him in Hiroshima [DOC]. They are named as Akira Iwanaga and Kuniyoshi Sato by [HIST] and by Pellegrino's APJJF article. Lloyd Parry interviewed both in 2005.
- **UNCERTAIN:** The forgotten-hanko story. It appears in Wikipedia and in a Japanese school English-reader translation (https://www.ryugasaki1-h.ibk.ed.jp/wysiwyg/file/download/1/1616), and is attributed to the Times and Telegraph, but I could not read those originals.
- **CONFIRMED:** He spoke at the UN in New York in 2006, at a screening of the documentary *Niju Hibaku* (English titles vary: "Twice Survived" / "Twice Bombed, Twice Survived"). Sources: [AFP2010], [AP2010], [ABC2010], [IND2010]. His memoir excerpt adds that in August 2006 he got his first passport and spoke at the UN and Columbia University [MEMOIR].
- **CONFIRMED:** After the war he worked as a translator for US/occupation forces, then as a teacher, then returned to Mitsubishi. Sources: [AP2010], [NYT2010], [HIST].
- **CONFIRMED:** He was certified as a Nagasaki hibakusha first. Double certification was announced on 24 March 2009 by Nagasaki city officials. Sources: [AP2009], [JT2009].
- **Nuance on the 1957 handbook (UNCERTAIN):** [JAWIKI] and Gizmodo Japan (https://www.gizmodo.jp/article/post_5295/) say his first handbook (Aug 1957) listed both cities, and that Hiroshima was dropped when the handbook was renewed. English Wikipedia says it recorded Nagasaki only.
- **CONFIRMED:** He died on 4 January 2010 of stomach cancer, aged 93, in a Nagasaki hospital. Sources: [AP2010], [AFP2010], [NYT2010]. [JAWIKI] gives the time as 5:38 a.m. Newsweek Japan's "6 January" is wrong.
- **CONFIRMED:** On 22 Dec 2009 James Cameron and Charles Pellegrino visited him in hospital. Sources: [AFP2010], [IND2010], [DOC].

## Q1. Wife and son in August 1945

- **CONFIRMED: he had a wife and an infant son in Nagasaki in August 1945.** Wikipedia's birth year of 1946 for Katsutoshi appears to be wrong.
  - [MEMOIR] (his own words): at the Hiroshima flash he saw "妻や我が子の顔" (the faces of my wife and my child).
  - [DOC]: in May 1945 he left "妻と生後3ヶ月の息子" (his wife and 3-month-old son) in Nagasaki, which puts the birth around February 1945.
  - [AFP2010]: "his second son – who survived the Nagasaki bombing as an infant" died of cancer in 2005.
  - [NYT2010], quoting the Independent: daughter Toshiko said her brother "died of cancer at age 59". A death in 2005 at 59 implies a birth in about 1945.
  - [HIST]: "their infant son, Katsutoshi".
  - Gizmodo Japan (weak) says he was 5 months old at the bombing.
- **CONFIRMED: the son's name was Katsutoshi (捷利), and he died in February 2005 of cancer that the family attributed to the bomb.** Sources: [DOC] ("2005年2月、息子の捷利さんが原爆症が原因で亡くなる"), [IND2010] ("His son Katsutoshi died of cancer in 2005 aged 59"), [AFP2010]. This death is what moved Yamaguchi to begin speaking publicly.
- **UNCERTAIN: was Katsutoshi the "second son"?** AFP says "second son" and that he was survived by "a son, two daughters". Wikipedia lists only three children. Don't call Katsutoshi his only son.
- **Wife's name Hisako: UNCERTAIN (secondary only).** It appears in Wikipedia and [HIST]; I found no Japanese primary source for it.
  - **CONFIRMED:** she survived the Nagasaki bombing and died in 2008, aged 88, of kidney and liver cancer. Daughter Toshiko said she was "soaked in black rain and was poisoned". Sources: [NYT2010] quoting the Independent, [IND2010].
- **CONFIRMED: both wife and son survived 9 August.** See the deaths in 2005 and 2008 above, and [AFP2010].
- **UNCERTAIN: what happened to them that day.**
  - Only [HIST] (probably drawing on the Times or Pellegrino) says his wife had gone out to find burn ointment for him and sheltered with the baby in a tunnel, that part of the house was wrecked, and that both had only superficial injuries.
  - Another popular claim, that he found them "in the rubble", is unsourced.
  - Gizmodo Japan (weak) says he entered the area near the hypocenter on 13 August to look for relatives.

## Q2. Hiroshima: location at 8:15, the night, and the return journey

- **CONFIRMED: about 3 km from the hypocenter, on his way to the Mitsubishi shipyard.** Sources: [DOC] (3 km, "造船所への出勤途中"), [IND2010] (3 km), [AP2009]/[JT2009] (in Hiroshima on business), [HIST].
  - **UNCERTAIN:** the exact distance. AFP says about 2 km, and the NYT says "less than 2 miles". Use "about 3 km".
- **Where exactly:**
  - **CONFIRMED (his own account plus [DOC]):** he was blown into a sweet-potato field (芋畑). [MEMOIR]: "爆風で私は芋畑に吹き飛ばされていた". [DOC]: "爆心地から3キロ地点の芋畑で被爆".
  - **Probable:** he had just come off a streetcar in the Eba district. [NYT2010] says "getting off a streetcar", and [JAWIKI] places it at the Eba streetcar stop (江波電停) on the Hiroshima Electric Railway's Eba line.
- **CONFIRMED:** he saw a B-29 and two small white parachutes. Sources: [MEMOIR] ("小さな白い落下傘がふたつ"), and [WEEK] quoting the Telegraph: "It dropped two parachutes".
- **CONFIRMED: injuries.** Severe burns to the left upper body, face and arms; temporary blindness; a ruptured eardrum and lasting deafness in the left ear. Sources: [DOC], [JAWIKI], [IND2010], [AP2009], [ABC2010] (via the Mainichi).
  - **UNCERTAIN:** whether one or both eardrums ruptured. [HIST] says both; the Japanese sources say the left.
- **The night of 6 August: CONFIRMED that he stayed in Hiroshima** ([AP2009], [JT2009]: "spent the night in the city").
  - **UNCERTAIN where:** "air-raid/bomb shelter" in [NYT2010] and [HIST], but "the garden of a company dormitory in the city" (市内の寮の庭) with his two colleagues in [DOC].
- **The river crossing: UNCERTAIN.** Only [HIST] says he had to swim through floating bodies at one river crossing.
  - **CONFIRMED (milder alternative):** in the 2006 documentary he wept describing bloated corpses floating in the city's rivers [IND2010].
- **The train: CONFIRMED that he left by evacuation train on the afternoon of 7 August and reached Nagasaki on 8 August.** Sources: [DOC] ("8月7日午後、避難列車に乗り、翌8日昼に長崎に到着"), [AFP2010] ("returned to Nagasaki two days later"), [NWJ] ("8日に列車で長崎に帰り"), [HIST] (overnight ride, arriving 8 Aug).
  - Wikipedia's "returned the following day" refers to his departure, not his arrival.
  - **UNCERTAIN:** arrival time. [DOC] says around midday; [HIST] says early morning.
- **The departure station, Kōi (己斐), now Nishi-Hiroshima: probable, not fully confirmed.** Sources: [JAWIKI] (citing his book) and the school reader. Pellegrino also describes trains leaving Kōi on 7 August.
- **Hospital treatment in Nagasaki on 8 August: CONFIRMED that he was treated.** Sources: HistoryNet / *Military History*, July 2009 ("returned to Nagasaki the next day for treatment": https://www.historynet.com/survivor-of-hiroshima-and-nagasaki-identified/), [HIST], Wikipedia (citing the Telegraph), the school reader.
  - **UNCERTAIN (single source, [HIST]):** that the doctor was a former classmate who didn't recognise him, and that his mother thought he was a ghost.

## Q3. Nagasaki, 9 August

- **CONFIRMED: an office at Mitsubishi's Nagasaki Shipyard, about 3 km from the hypocenter.** Sources: [JAWIKI] (長崎三菱造船所の事務所), [DOC] ("造船所に出勤"), [AFP2010] ("his work place, about three kilometers from the epicentre"), [NYT2010] ("his Nagasaki office").
  - **UNCERTAIN:** the specific building. Pellegrino says it was the "main office of Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, on the west bank".
- **CONFIRMED:** he was reporting on Hiroshima to his superior, who didn't believe him. The superior is described as a company director in [HIST] and as a section chief (課長) in Gizmodo Japan. Sources: [WEEK] (Times obituary), [NYT2010], [DOC].
- **What he did at the flash:**
  - **UNCERTAIN:** that he "dropped to the floor". This appears only in [HIST] and in Biography/Yahoo, which draws on Pellegrino.
  - **CONFIRMED wording you can use:** "the same white light filled the room" ([NYT2010], quoting the Independent).
  - [WEEK] (Times/Telegraph): "the whole office was blown over", and he was "again knocked unconscious".
  - The "reinforced stairwell" detail comes from Pellegrino. Avoid it.
- **CONFIRMED (secondary):** afterwards he had fever, vomiting and hair loss; he was ill and delirious at the time of the 15 August surrender broadcast. Sources: [HIST], [WEEK], [IND2010] (went bald).

## Q4. Verified short quotes

1. "Suddenly the same white light filled the room. I thought the mushroom cloud had followed me from Hiroshima." **CONFIRMED.** Independent, March 2009, quoted in [NYT2010] and [IND2010]; [WEEK] also gives it via the Telegraph/Times.
2. "It dropped two parachutes. Suddenly it was like a flash of magnesium, a great flash in the sky, and I was blown over." **CONFIRMED.** Telegraph obituary via [WEEK]. His own memoir also describes a "magnesium-coloured" explosion (マグネシウムの色をした大爆発) [MEMOIR].
3. "My double radiation exposure is now an official government record. It can tell the younger generation the horrifying history of the atomic bombings even after I die." **CONFIRMED.** Mainichi, March 2009, quoted in [AP2009], [JT2009], [AP2010], [ABC2010].
4. "I could have died on either of those days. Everything that follows is a bonus." **CONFIRMED.** Mainichi Daily News interview, August 2009, quoted in [NYT2010].
5. "I think this will be my last lecture. I hope the baton will be passed to other people." **CONFIRMED.** Nagasaki seminar, June 2009; [AFP2010] via RTÉ and SBS.
6. "Please pass on my experience to future generations." (to James Cameron) **CONFIRMED.** Sources: [IND2010], [WEEK].
   - Related: daughter Toshiko reported him saying "My mission is over" [AFP2010], and 「私の役目は終わった。後はあなたに託したい」 ("My role is over; I entrust the rest to you") [JAWIKI].
7. "I didn't know what had happened... I think I fainted for a while. When I opened my eyes, everything was dark..." **UNCERTAIN.** The Times, via [HIST] only. His memoir confirms that he lost consciousness [MEMOIR].
8. "Having experienced atomic bombings twice and survived, it is my destiny to talk about it." **UNCERTAIN.** Found only in [HIST] and Yahoo/Biography.
9. "The reason that I hate the atomic bomb is because of what it does to the dignity of human beings." **UNCERTAIN.**
   - Wikipedia cites only the print *Military History* magazine (vol. 26, no. 5, Aug–Sep 2009, p. 8).
   - The magazine's online July 2009 item does not contain it.
   - I found no interview or transcript; later articles repeat it from Wikipedia ("according to several books and media outlets").
   - Avoid it, or attribute it cautiously.
10. "I can't understand why the world cannot understand the agony of the nuclear bombs. How can they keep developing these weapons?" **UNCERTAIN.** Attributed to the Independent (2009, through his daughter); I could not read the original.

Family quotes useful for tone (all **CONFIRMED** via [NYT2010], quoting the Independent):
- Toshiko: "He was so healthy, he thought it would have been unfair to people who were really sick."
- Toshiko: "Afterwards he was fine. We hardly noticed he was a survivor."

## Q5. Times, aircraft and death tolls

- **CONFIRMED: Hiroshima, 8:15 a.m., 6 Aug 1945, B-29 Enola Gay, "Little Boy".**
  - Hiroshima City: https://www.city.hiroshima.lg.jp/english/peace/1029920/1009857.html
  - Atomic Heritage Foundation: https://ahf.nuclearmuseum.org/ahf/history/bombings-hiroshima-and-nagasaki-1945/
- **CONFIRMED: Nagasaki, 11:02 a.m., 9 Aug 1945, B-29 Bockscar, "Fat Man".**
  - Nagasaki Atomic Bomb Museum: "At 11:02 a.m. on August 9, 1945..." https://nagasakipeace.jp/en/visit/abm
  - Atomic Heritage Foundation: released at 10:58, exploded 43 seconds later.
- **CONFIRMED: deaths by the end of 1945. Hiroshima about 140,000; Nagasaki 73,884.**
  - **Hiroshima City:** "Estimates place the number of dead by the end of December 1945... at roughly 140,000." https://www.city.hiroshima.lg.jp/english/peace/1029875/1010074.html
  - **Nagasaki City:** "by the end of December 1945, 73,884 of its people had been killed, and 74,909 had been injured." This is an estimate from the July 1950 report. https://nagasakipeace.jp/en/reference/materials/kids/higai.html
  - **Most conservative, RERF:** acute deaths within 2–4 months were Hiroshima 90,000–166,000 and Nagasaki 60,000–80,000. https://www.rerf.or.jp/en/faq/
  - Safe narration: "by the end of 1945, an estimated 140,000 people in Hiroshima and more than 70,000 in Nagasaki had died."

## Q6. Distance and train journey

- **Straight-line distance: about 300 km (about 190 miles). CONFIRMED.** [AP2010] gives "about 190 miles"; my great-circle calculation gives about 300 km.
- **Rail route (my inference; I found no source naming the trains he took):**
  - Sanyō Main Line from Kōi/Hiroshima to Moji. The line runs Kōbe–Moji, and the Kanmon railway tunnel opened in 1942 ([en.wikipedia: Kanmon Railway Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanmon_Railway_Tunnel)).
  - Kagoshima Main Line from Moji to Tosu.
  - Nagasaki Main Line from Tosu to Nagasaki.
  - From modern kilometre markers that is about 224 + 101 + 125 ≈ 450 km.
  - The wartime alignment differed slightly; for example, the Nagasaki-end section ran via Nagayo before 1972. Say "about 450 km by rail".
- **Journey time: about 20 hours, based on his account. UNCERTAIN in detail.** [DOC] gives departure on the afternoon of 7 Aug and arrival around midday on 8 Aug; [HIST] says "overnight". I found no 1945 timetable; post-bombing service was irregular.

## Q7. Framing to avoid, and family wishes

- **"Luckiest or unluckiest man" / "Lucky Yamaguchi":**
  - The Times headline, 25 Mar 2009 (Wikipedia citation).
  - The Independent obituary: "Reporters never knew whether to call Tsutomu Yamaguchi the luckiest or unluckiest man alive" [IND2010].
  - The Daily Mail, 24 Mar 2009: "Meet 'Lucky' Yamaguchi" (https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/70543).
- **The BBC *QI* episode (17 Dec 2010) introduced him as "The Unluckiest Man in the World", to audience laughter.** The Japanese Embassy protested (7 Jan 2011), and the BBC and Talkback Thames apologised (22 Jan 2011). **CONFIRMED.** Sources: [KYODO2011]; Chortle: https://www.chortle.co.uk/news/2011/01/23/12608/bbc_apologises_for_qi_atom_bomb_jokes
- **Daughter Toshiko Yamasaki [KYODO2011]:**
  - "I cannot forgive (the quiz show) as it looked down on my father's experiences when the world is moving toward nuclear disarmament."
  - The family had "laughingly talked about her father being unlucky", but "it is a different story when (my father) was treated in that way in Britain, a nuclear-capable nation."
- **How he and his family wanted the story used: as testimony for passing the experience on and for nuclear abolition.** Sources:
  - quotes 3, 5 and 6 above;
  - Toshiko: "I thanked my father for leaving us with the treasure that was his effort to call for world peace" [AFP2010];
  - for decades he declined to campaign out of consideration for sicker survivors ([NYT2010]; Gizmodo Japan: 「もっと重い病気の人にすまないから」, "because I'd feel bad toward people with worse illnesses").
- **Suggestion:** frame him as a witness and storyteller (語り部, the Nagasaki mayor's "precious storyteller" [AP2010]), not as lucky or unlucky.

## Errors and caveats in commonly repeated material

- **Wikipedia:** Katsutoshi's birth year "1946" conflicts with Yamaguchi's own memoir and with [DOC], which both point to early 1945.
- **Wikipedia:** "returned to Nagasaki the following day" (7 Aug) means he left on 7 Aug; most accounts have him arriving on 8 Aug.
- **[IND2010]** calls Toshiko his "sister"; she is his daughter.
- **[NWJ]** gives the death date as 6 Jan; the correct date is 4 Jan 2010.
- **Pellegrino-derived details are weak sources:** the stairwell, the ditch with thumbs in his ears, and the woman with a parasol vanishing. His own memoir does mention a woman with a parasol who ran and hid in a nearby creek, but not that she vanished.
