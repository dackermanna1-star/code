# LIMINAL MOSCOW — A Voxel Winter Walking Simulator (original creative brief)

Reference photos supplied with the brief: snowy Soviet panel-block courtyards at night, heavy
snowfall under orange sodium lamps, snow-buried parked cars, a playground lit by a single lamp,
a purple-pink dusk sky over 9-storey blocks, lit windows in warm yellow and cold blue, a lone
pedestrian far away on a snowy driveway.

## Core concept
A first-person, atmospheric voxel walking simulator set in Moscow, Russia, during the winter of the
late 2000s (approximately 2007–2009). The game should feel like a half-forgotten memory of a winter
that never seems to end: quiet, melancholic, strangely nostalgic, and subtly unsettling.

The player wanders through snow-covered Soviet-era residential districts, empty courtyards,
abandoned playgrounds, enormous apartment complexes, frozen parks, and deserted streets beneath a
pale winter sky. The world should feel simultaneously familiar and alien, as if the player is
exploring a real city remembered through a dream.

The experience is not about completing objectives, fighting enemies, or solving complex puzzles.
It is about walking, observing, listening, getting lost, and experiencing the profound loneliness
and beauty of an ordinary winter evening in Moscow.

It should evoke the feeling of walking home alone at dusk in 2008, carrying an old mobile phone in
your pocket, with snow falling under orange streetlights and the distant hum of a city that seems
to have forgotten you exist.

## 1. Visual direction and voxel art style
Every environment, building, street, tree, vehicle, prop, and interior should feel constructed from
small, tangible cubic forms. Avoid colorful, cartoonish, playful voxel graphics. Use a restrained,
realistic, low-resolution art direction emphasizing environmental scale, lighting, geometry, and
atmosphere.
- Large, blocky Soviet apartment buildings with repetitive windows, weathered concrete facades,
  faded pastel panels, exposed pipes, balconies, snow-covered rooftops.
- Thick layers of voxel snow on sidewalks, parked cars, benches, playground equipment, fences, rooftops.
- Bare trees with angular branches emerging from snow-covered ground.
- Old Russian cars from the 1990s and 2000s, partially buried beneath snow.
- Dimly illuminated apartment windows, some warm yellow, others dark.
- Worn stairwells, chipped paint, tiled underground passages, rusted metal doors, Soviet-era
  signage, flickering fluorescent lights.
- Realistic proportions: enormous blocks and open spaces → isolation, human insignificance.
Limited winter palette: dirty whites, blue-gray snow shadows, desaturated cyan, faded concrete gray,
muted brown, dull green, occasional warm amber light. The contrast between cold outdoors and warm
indoor light is a defining feature. Voxel geometry visible without being distracting. Soft
volumetric fog, subtle dithering, restrained AO, low bloom, careful lighting — like a forgotten PC
game from 2008 reconstructed through a dream.

## 2. Setting: Moscow 2007–2009
Fictionalized but culturally authentic Moscow residential outskirts / Soviet microdistricts. No
smartphones, modern LED advertising, futuristic architecture.
- **Soviet residential microdistricts:** vast repeating concrete blocks, courtyards, passages,
  entrances; frosted windows, enclosed balconies, metal entrance doors with worn intercom panels,
  mailboxes, dim hallways, snow-covered playgrounds and sandboxes, parked Ladas, TV light
  flickering through windows, occasional silhouettes behind curtains.
- **Empty winter streets:** wide roads with tire tracks in slush, long amber pools of streetlight;
  sparse traffic — an old bus, trolleybus or car passing in the distance, sound fading into fog.
- **Frozen parks and playgrounds:** bare birches, old playground equipment, frozen ponds, benches,
  winding paths; peaceful by day, disorienting at night when paths disappear into fog.
- **Apartment interiors:** stairwells, entrance halls, landings, elevators, corridors; worn
  linoleum, patterned wallpaper, exposed concrete, metal railings, weak incandescent bulbs, distant
  TV sounds behind closed doors.
- **Small shops and kiosks:** grocery stores, kiosks, vending machines; warm light spilling onto
  snow, fridges hum, fluorescent lights buzz, muffled Russian conversation. Comforting without
  removing the loneliness.

## 3. Atmosphere and liminality
Between states: day/night, home/destination, civilization/abandonment, memory/reality. No horror
tropes, jump scares, monsters. Create liminality through: enormous spaces with few people;
repetitive architecture; long corridors with no visible destination; empty lit playgrounds;
buildings disappearing into low cloud; subway platforms where the next train takes unusually long;
familiar streets slightly unfamiliar after a lighting/weather change; distant sounds with no visible
source; unnaturally quiet courtyards where even the wind stops; roads, staircases, tunnels that
continue farther than expected. Rare, understated, ambiguous anomalies: a distant building that
seems closer on the second visit, a corridor longer than before, a lit window visible from
impossible angles. The player should never know whether anything supernatural happened.

## 4. Snow, weather, environment
Fine snow drifting through streetlights; occasional heavy snowfall reducing visibility; snow
accumulating on surfaces; snow swirling around corners and through courtyards; footprints behind
the player gradually filled by fresh snow. Low fog between distant blocks, buildings fading into a
pale gray horizon, headlights diffusing through snowfall, roads fading into white. Footsteps change
with surface: soft snow, crunchy ice, wet slush, concrete, metal stairs, hollow underground passages.
Physical presence in the cold without an intrusive survival system.

## 5. Audio
The world lives through distant, fragmented environmental audio rather than constant music: wind
between blocks, boots crunching snow, distant traffic on wet roads, muffled metro rumble underground,
buzzing fluorescents, an old elevator somewhere, distant dogs, faint Russian conversations behind
doors, an indistinct TV, a trolleybus on the avenue, creaking playground equipment, occasional
footsteps of someone unseen, distant metro announcements. Realistic reverb and occlusion between
streets, stairwells, tunnels, interiors. Silence is intentional: sometimes everything fades but
breathing and footsteps. Music: minimal, sparse, mostly diegetic/ambient — muffled electronic tones,
soft analog drones, degraded textures, melancholy fragments like cheap late-2000s electronics.
Rarely a distant radio/TV with a fragment of period Russian pop or an indistinct broadcast.

## 6. Gameplay
First person, natural movement, restrained head bob, realistic acceleration, smooth camera.
Leisurely pace; sprint/jump/combat/inventory absent or minimal. Interactions: walk and look; open
selected doors; enter buildings and public spaces; ride escalators and elevators; inspect objects;
sit on benches; read posters, signs, ads, handwritten notes; use a period mobile phone; wait for a
bus or metro train; notice subtle changes when revisiting places. No quest markers, minimap, HUD,
objective indicators or notifications. Navigation by landmarks, street signs, familiarity,
curiosity. A player who stops under a streetlight and watches the snow for minutes should feel
something meaningful even if nothing happens.

## 7. Late-2000s nostalgia
Older phones with small screens and keypads; CRT TVs through windows; some early flat screens;
period Russian ads and posters; old cars and buses; faded shop signs and printed price labels;
payphones and aging vending machines; worn monitors in office windows; old plastic furniture,
patterned wallpaper; authentic Russian signage and Cyrillic typography; the everyday transition
between Soviet infrastructure and late-2000s consumer culture — never a collection of clichés.

## 8. Environmental storytelling
Children's mittens on a snowy bench; a stairwell with one working light among broken ones; a shop
that just closed for the night; a snow-covered car with a warm engine and fogged windows; a
handwritten notice on an entrance; a lit kitchen window where someone cooks dinner; a bus stop with
a faded timetable and one discarded ticket; a row of dark windows with one lit room; an open gate to
a courtyard that feels familiar despite never being visited. No rewards or explanations required.
Themes: loneliness, memory, passage of time, urban anonymity, childhood nostalgia, the strange
comfort of being alone in a familiar place.

## 9. World design
Seamless interconnected districts: a large residential microdistrict, a main road with sparse
traffic, a frozen park, several apartment interiors, a bus terminal or trolleybus stop, a dim
industrial/commercial edge, transitional spaces (courtyards, stairwells, underpasses, passages).
Recognizable landmarks plus visually similar spaces for gentle disorientation. Emerging from narrow
corridors into enormous open areas. Distant buildings visible through fog for direction; the city
feels much larger than the explorable area.

## 11. Interface
Almost invisible: no HUD. Minimal pause menu inspired by old PC games and late-2000s operating
systems; restrained typography, muted colors.

## 12. Performance
Modular voxel assets, reusable architectural pieces, efficient rendering, LOD, controlled fog and
lighting. Consistent frame pacing, smooth movement, good AO, efficient snow, subtle volumetric light,
convincing indoor/outdoor audio transitions, dense detail near the player, distance fog hiding
level boundaries.

## 13. Emotional target
A long solitary walk through a Moscow winter between reality and memory: the quiet melancholy of an
empty city at dusk; late-2000s Eastern European urban nostalgia; the warmth of lit windows against
the cold; the strange emptiness of familiar public spaces after dark; the distant presence of
millions of lives beyond view; remembering a place that may never have existed exactly as
remembered. The player is never the center of the world — the city is the protagonist. Beautiful,
lonely, grounded, quietly haunting; as if you could keep walking through the snow forever.
