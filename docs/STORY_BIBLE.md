# What Derry Keeps — Story Bible

> ⚠️ **SPOILERS FOR THE WHOLE SERIES.** This is the writers' room document.
> Play Episode One before reading.

## Premise

Autumn 1991. Augusta "Gus" Brennan, 14, moves with her newly divorced mother
Claire into her grandmother's house at 9 Larch Street in Derry, Maine — the
town Gus was born in and left at five. Mémère (Yvette Thibodeau) has dementia
and keeps calling Gus "Junie." Gus records sounds on a cassette recorder she
has always had. On those tapes, under the hiss, a girl's voice is waiting.

Nobody in Derry remembers June Brennan, Gus's older sister, taken on the
Quiet night of 1982. Not her parents. Not Gus.

## Tone

Small-town horror with a warm heart. Real teenage life — lockers, lunch tables,
crushes, embarrassment, friendships that matter more than anything — running
alongside a dread that builds by inches. Humor is how these characters cope.
Horror is mostly what's *missing*: a plate set for nobody, a name that slides
off the mind, a town that goes to bed at eight. The supernatural is shown
sparingly and is never fully explained in Episode One.

## The Town

Derry is the town from the stories: the Kenduskeag Stream and the downtown
canal, the Barrens, the ruined Standpipe on its hill (collapsed in the storm of
'85 — locals say "the flood"), the Aladdin theater, Bassey Park and its Paul
Bunyan statue, the Derry Public Library with its glass corridor. Its history
has a rhythm of catastrophe (the Kitchener Ironworks explosion, the Bradley
Gang, the Black Spot fire, the child murders of '57–'58, the '85 storm) that
locals don't discuss. **This story is original**: we never use the creature
from the novel. Derry has more than one hunger.

## The Lore (secret)

**The Quiet.** On the second Saturday of every October, the adults of Derry
feel an overwhelming urge to be home by dark, draw the curtains, put out every
light, and sleep. Most don't know they're doing it. Kids call it "Quiet Night."
Stores close early. In 1991 it falls on **Saturday, October 12**.

**The Keeping.** On Quiet night a single old brass ship's lantern — the Lamp —
burns green somewhere in town (usually at the Standpipe). Whoever is outside
and goes to its light is **Kept**: taken into the dark under and behind Derry.
The Lamp calls each person in the voice of whatever they want most. The Kept
don't die; they're held in the town's memory — the *Hollow* — unaging. (Ep 2+.)

**The Forgetting.** By morning Derry forgets the Kept: minds smooth over the gap
and invent "patches" (Claire believes Mémère "had a little sister June who died
of diphtheria"). Photos fade over weeks. Written names fade unless rewritten.
Carved marks stay, but locals' eyes slide off them. **Magnetic tape keeps
voices.** Forcing a memory causes nosebleeds and headaches; remembering for
years erodes the mind (Mémère's dementia; Teague's drinking).
Leftover grief shows as **phantom habits**: Claire sets four plates and checks
the empty back seat; Dot makes a grilled cheese at 3:15 for a boy she never
had; Nate wrote "Girl in the Static" about a girl he's "never met"; Danny
Brennan calls at 2 a.m. crying and says "tell your sister—".

**Who remembers.** Anyone who *witnesses* a Keeping — sees the person walk into
the light — remembers that person. That's why the Society remembers, why
Teague remembers his daughter Rose (1961), why Cal remembers his friend Toby
Mercer (1986), and why Mémère remembers June. People with "unmoored" minds
(dementia, fever, very small children) catch glimpses. Gus, at five, was asleep
— she never saw June go.

**The cost.** The Lamp must be fed. If the chosen one is pulled away, it takes
the nearest heart instead — or someone who walks into the light in their
place. (Teague pulled Rose out of the light in 1961; it took his wife, then came
back for Rose an hour later.)

**The Lantern Society** (est. 1881) is Derry's genteel civic club — Canal Days,
the Lantern Float, town beautification. Its inner circle keeps the Quiet: they
choose who will be outside (drifters, runaways, foster kids, troublemakers —
"the ones nobody will miss"), arrange the lure, light the Lamp, witness, and
clean up afterward (the "casserole committee" visits the family and removes
belongings). They believe they are protecting the town from something worse,
and they may be right. Members wear a small brass lantern pin.
Members in 1991: **Constance Ashgrove** (president), **Principal Dale
Fenwick**, **Officer Gary Lindqvist** (Brett's father), **Mrs. Fennimore**
(librarian), Dr. Halvorsen, Pastor Wills, the head of nursing at Derry Home
Hospital (who recruited Claire back — Ep 2). **Mémère** was a member 1946–1982.
Minutes record each year as one line: `Kept.` and a blacked-out name.
1982: `Kept. (Y.T. abstained.)`

**The Starving Winter** (1880, founding legend): the town would have starved;
a girl walked into the snow with a lantern and in the morning the deer came
back and the river thawed, "and we never said her name again, because that was
the price." The first Kept.

**1982.** June Brennan, 14, a Derry High freshman, had been investigating the
Quiet with her best friend Paige Whitcomb and her boyfriend Nate Fortier. The
Society chose June because she was getting close. Mémère abstained and did
nothing else. On Quiet night the Lamp lured June outside with the voice and
shape of five-year-old Gus in the yard (Gus was asleep in bed). June went out to
bring her sister in. Mémère, at the Standpipe as a witness, saw June taken; she
quit the Society, hid a box of June's things in the attic behind a loose board
("NE PAS JETER"), and has been coming apart ever since. Claire and Danny moved
the family to Portland in 1983 without knowing why. Gus kept June's cassette
recorder; the family's patched memory says Danny gave it to her.

**Ep 1's last tape.** On Sunday morning a brand-new 1991 Maxell (an anachronism
an attentive player catches — June's handwriting on a tape that didn't exist in
1982) is on Gus's pillow. June, from the Hollow, has learned to reach her.
Or something wearing June has. *Mémère's warning: "Don't open the door. Not
even for her."*

## Characters

**Gus (Augusta Marie Brennan), 14.** Born in Derry, raised in Portland since
six. Observant, dry, guarded; collects "found sounds" on cassette and makes
sound collages; hums a song she doesn't know the source of when she's anxious
("À la claire fontaine" — June used to sing it to her). The player decides
whether she's warm or sharp, brave or careful, honest or sly. Her parents'
divorce made her good at reading rooms and bad at asking for things.

**Claire Thibodeau, 44 (Mom).** ER nurse, newly divorced, back in her mother's
house because there was no other option. Sardonic, loving, brittle; secretly
smoking again. Left Derry at 18, came back to raise a family, left again in
'83 "because I needed out" — she can't say why. Sets four plates. Gets
nosebleeds when pushed about June. On Quiet night she becomes a sleepwalking
stranger.

**Mémère (Yvette Thibodeau), 72.** Franco-American (French phrases:
*ma belle, mon Dieu, viens ici, fais attention, tabarnouche*), former
seamstress, Lantern Society member for 36 years. Dementia: funny and cutting
when lucid, eerie when not. Calls Gus "Junie." Sings "À la claire fontaine"
(*Il y a longtemps que je t'aime, jamais je ne t'oublierai* — "I've loved you a
long time; I will never forget you"). Plays Kaiser. Her cardigan is buttoned
wrong. Wears her lantern pin always. Carries 1982 like a stone.

**Danny Brennan, 47 (Dad).** Sound engineer in Boston, remarried (Trish,
pregnant). Phone only. Warm, distractible, absent.

**June Marie Brennan, 14 forever.** Bossy, funny, fearless, a big sister to her
bones. Voice on tapes: "This is June Brennan, and I'm going to find out what
happens on Quiet night."

**Teddy (Theodora) Okafor, 14.** Moved from Houston two years ago (mother:
geology professor at UMaine Orono; father: engineer at the paper mill). Loud,
funny, nerdy (Star Trek, astronomy, her ham radio — call sign KA1TDO).
Lonely in a town that's been cold to her family. Flaws: jealous, talks over
people, embellishes. Secret: last Quiet night her radio picked up a flat voice
reading names; she taped it and told no one. Her parents are fighting; her mom
may take a job back in Texas. The Lamp would call her in her late grandmother's
voice.

**Cal (Calvin) Ashgrove, 14.** Sloane's twin. Old Family. Pale, formal, morbid,
brilliant, socially reckless; carries "the Derry Book" (marbled notebook of
town disasters). Secret: at eight he followed his friend Toby Mercer out on
Quiet night 1986 and saw him taken; he rewrites Toby's name in the Derry Book
every day so he won't forget. He's been reading his mother's minutes. Flaw: he
wants *proof* more than he wants people safe — he'd photograph the Keeping
rather than stop it, unless Gus has made him care.

**Sloane Ashgrove, 14.** Queen bee, cheer squad, cruelty as armor. Knows more
about the Society than Cal realizes; is being groomed to join at sixteen.
Protects Cal in ways he never sees. Respects bluntness, despises begging. Can
become Gus's rival, wary ally, or something complicated.

**Wes (Wesley) Pike, 15.** Foster kid placed with the Dunmores on Kansas Street
in August after running from two placements. Terse, deadpan, unexpectedly
gentle; draws monsters and maps ("places in Derry that feel wrong"); feeds a
one-eyed stray tom he calls the Admiral in the Barrens. Wants one thing: his
mother. **This year's chosen.** The lure: a typed note — "Meet me at the old
Standpipe Saturday, October 12, after dark. Come alone. — Mom" — delivered by
Brett, typed on the Society's typewriter (its lowercase "e" is chipped).

**Brett Lindqvist, 17.** Golden boy quarterback, Principal Fenwick's nephew,
Officer Lindqvist's son. Charming to adults, cruel to anyone below him. The
Society's errand boy; he thinks Quiet night is a hazing ritual that scares
unwanted kids out of town. He's about to learn otherwise.

**Ms. Paige Whitcomb, 23.** First-year English teacher, Derry High class of
'86. June's best friend; forgot her. Flinches at the name Brennan; has
blackouts and nosebleeds she hides. Assigns "The Lottery." Ally in Ep 2.

**Mr. Teague, 68.** Night janitor since 1957. Korean War vet. Daughter Rose
Kept in 1961. Keeps the Wall in the boiler room: one name per year in grease
pencil, retraced nightly. Knows the cost. Drinks. Plays chess with Cal.

**Nate Fortier, 23.** Low Tide Records. June's boyfriend; forgot her. His band
the Static Hours plays "Girl in the Static." Kind; heartbreakable.

**Dot Carrier, 64.** Dot's Diner. Gossip, pie, free coffee for new kids. Son
Daniel Kept in 1971; she "never had children."

**Constance Ashgrove, 48.** The face of the Society. Gracious, cold, certain.

**Hannah Gagnon, 8.** Next door. Knows the rhyme. Says what grown-ups won't.

**Mrs. Fennimore** (librarian, Society), **Principal Fenwick** (Society),
**Officer Lindqvist** (Society), **Mr. Paulsen** (history), **the Dunmores**
(Wes's foster parents, decent and overwhelmed).

## The Rhyme (Derry kids' jump-rope game, "Lantern")

> Lantern, lantern, burning low,
> Who goes walking? Who's to know?
> Close your eyes and hold your breath,
> Never answer, never guess.
> If it calls you, let it call —
> Derry keeps us, one and all.

Rules encoded: don't look at the light; hold your breath when it passes; never
answer when it calls your name; never say whose voice it is.

### Saving Wes: anchors (need 2+)
1. A recording of Wes's own voice (Ch 3 Barrens, or Ch 4) played to him.
2. At least one ally physically with Gus who knows the rules (Teddy, Cal with
   high trust, or Sloane with high respect).
3. Proof the note is fake (saw the chipped "e" in the minutes book at the
   party AND saw the note) — told to Wes.
4. Wes's trust in Gus is high (he hesitates when she calls him).
With one anchor Wes falters but walks on. With none he doesn't hear her.

### Outcomes
- **Wes Kept** (failed or stayed home).
- **Brett Kept** (Wes saved; Gus stepped aside and let the light find Brett).
- **Mémère Kept** (Wes saved; Gus stood her ground — Mémère walked into the
  light in her place: "Pas elle. Moi.").
Episode Two opens differently for each, and remembers who Gus told, who stood
with her, what she recorded, and whether she opened the window.
