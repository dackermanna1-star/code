# Moscow Metro outskirts station, about 2008: reference digest

Tags used below: **[V]** means checked against a source in this session. **[K]** means general knowledge I believe is reliable. **[est.]** means my own estimate or a suggested value for building; use it where a plausible number is needed.

## 0. Corrections to the brief
- **Tokens were not being phased out in 2008.** Moscow withdrew its metro tokens in **January–February 1999** [V]. Saint Petersburg kept tokens much longer, which is likely where the brief got the idea. In 2008 the change underway was from **magnetic-stripe paper tickets** to **contactless "Ультралайт" paper tickets** (MIFARE Ultralight). Contactless tickets were introduced on 1 Feb 2007, and in early 2008 they replaced the 1-, 2- and 5-trip magnetic tickets [V]. A coin or token slot on a turnstile would be an anachronism. A dusty leftover token in a drawer or under a radiator would work as an Easter egg.
- Escalators are uncommon at 1970s–80s shallow stations. Many have **stairs only**. If you want an escalator, make it a short 3-lane unit (see §8).

## 1. Station types and dimensions
**A. "Сороконожка" ("centipede"), 1960s to early 1970s, prefabricated concrete, cut-and-cover** [V]
- Three spans (3-пролётная), one island platform, 2 rows of **40 columns, 4.0 m column pitch**
- Column width **0.5 m** (2 voxels), spacing between column-row axes **5.9 m**, distance between track axes **12.9 m**, platform width **10 m**, ceiling height about **4 m**
- Platform length about **155 m** for 8 cars [K]. At 0.25 m voxels that is 620 voxels. Consider a 5-car platform of about 100 m with a visible end, or a repeating section.

**B. 1980s shallow column station with 6.5 m pitch** (example: Тёплый Стан, 1987) [V]
- **26 pairs (52 columns), 6.5 m pitch**, so 25 × 6.5 ≈ **162.5 m**. This is where the "~162 m" figure comes from.
- Depth about 8 m, no surface vestibule, exits through underground passages [V]
- Finishes at Тёплый Стан: white marble columns, large **red-brown ceramic tiles** on the track walls, grey and black granite floor, lamps set in niches in the ceiling [V]
- Bibirevo-type (1990s) has 22 rows of columns [V]

**C. Single-vault shallow station (односводчатая мелкого заложения)**
- One low vault on vertical walls that stand on a base slab, with no columns [V]
- Clear span about 18–20 m [est.]
- Lamps are hidden in a cornice along the vault springing and bounce off a white vault (indirect lighting). The effect is airy, cave-like and very reverberant.

**Common to all types** [K]
- Platform height **1.10 m** above the rail head
- Platform edge about **1.45 m** from the track axis. The car is 2.67 m wide, so the gap to the car is about 0.11 m.
- Track gauge 1520 mm, about 6 voxels
- Track wall about 2.0–2.3 m from the track axis
- The **third rail** runs on the track-wall side, away from the platform, under a wooden or plastic cover. Its top is about 0.16 m above the running rail.
- The platform edge overhangs a recess under the platform about 0.4–0.5 m deep (подплатформенная ниша)
- No tactile edge strip in 2008. Announcements do mention a "ограничительная линия" (limit line) [V], so draw a thin lighter granite band about 0.5 m from the edge.

## 3. Lighting
- Sorokonozhka stations: continuous rows of **fluorescent tubes** in recessed troughs, or slots between ceiling ribs. The light is cool and slightly green: `#E6F0EA`, roughly 4000–5000 K.
- 1980s stations: lamps in ceiling niches or decorative fixtures, cool white `#EEF2F4`.
- Vestibules and passages: fluorescent fixtures, some with a failing tube that flickers and hums at 100 Hz plus its harmonics.
- Platform illuminance about 100–150 lux [est.]. Tunnels show as near-black mouths with sparse warm points.

## 4. Signage and typography
- **Station name on the track walls:** individual metal capital letters about **0.4–0.5 m tall**, mounted at 2.5–3 m height, repeated 2–4 times along each wall. Use a Soviet geometric or condensed grotesque, with custom lettering per station. Example: **«УЛИЦА СТРОИТЕЛЕЙ»**, a fictional name. [K]
- **Hanging lightboxes** (the 2000s style before the 2013 redesign) [K/est.]: about 2.0–2.4 × 0.4–0.5 m, white face `#F3F3EF`, black capitals `#1A1A1A`, arrow, and a small line-colour mark.
  - **ВЫХОД В ГОРОД** = Exit to the city. Sub-lines such as «к улице Строителей» (to Builders St.), «к автобусам» (to the buses), «к кинотеатру "Комета"» (to the "Kometa" cinema).
  - **К ПОЕЗДАМ** = To the trains
  - **ПЕРЕХОД НА СТАНЦИЮ «…»** = Transfer to station "…". Rare at an outskirts station.
  - **ВХОД** = Entrance · **ВЫХОД** = Exit · **ВХОДА НЕТ** = No entry · **ВЫХОДА НЕТ** = No exit
  - **В СТОРОНУ ЦЕНТРА** = Toward the centre. A direction board lists the next stations in a column.
  - **КАССА** = Ticket office · **МИЛИЦИЯ** = Police. 2008 is still the милиция era; police wear grey-blue uniforms.
- **Platform end:** «ПРОХОД ЗАПРЕЩЁН» (No passage) on the end gate. A driver's marker plate «ОСТАНОВКА ПЕРВОГО ВАГОНА» (first-car stop) on the track wall. A convex mirror for the driver.
- **Inside the car:** «НЕ ПРИСЛОНЯТЬСЯ» (Do not lean) on the door glass. «МЕСТА ДЛЯ ИНВАЛИДОВ, ПОЖИЛЫХ ЛЮДЕЙ И ПАССАЖИРОВ С ДЕТЬМИ» (Seats for the disabled, elderly and passengers with children). An intercom grille labelled «СВЯЗЬ С МАШИНИСТОМ» (Intercom to driver). A paper line map above the doors. Ad stickers above the windows.

## 5. Interval clock (интервальные часы)
- Mounted **above the tunnel mouth** at the departure end of the platform. It is meant for drivers, but everyone looks at it. [V]
- **Left half shows the current time. Right half shows the interval since the previous train departed.** The interval resets when the departing train enters the tunnel. [V]
- Introduced on 7 Sep 1947. Dial faces were later replaced by digital displays. All clocks are synchronised from the Central Electric Clock Station (ЦЭЧС). [V]
- Build values [est.]: black housing `#0B0B0B`, about 1.6 × 0.35 m. Red 7-segment digits `#FF3A1E`, about 0.15–0.2 m tall, with a faint glow. Example readings: `23:41` | `6:12`. At night the right side climbs slowly, which suits an empty-platform scene.

## 6. Entrances, pavilions and the red "М"
- Outskirts stations usually have **no surface building**. You reach them by stairs down into a pedestrian underpass (подземный переход). The underpass holds kiosks (flowers, phones, newspapers, shawarma, shoe repair) and leads to an underground vestibule.
- Where there is a pavilion: a low concrete and glass box with aluminium-framed glazing and «МЕТРО» in large red letters along the roof edge. [K]
- **The big red М:** a sans-serif "М" with slightly flared or angled stems, about 1.0–1.5 m tall, on a 3–4 m pylon or the pavilion, lit from inside. Red `#D8261C`, glowing `#FF3B2F` at night. Often paired with a lightbox carrying the station name. The unified Lebedev "М" only came in 2014, so 2008 shows mixed variants.
- **Doors:** heavy glass **pendulum doors** that swing both ways, in wood or aluminium frames with brass push plates. People hold them for the person behind. They close with a deep "whump", about 80 Hz [est.].
- **Winter details:** a warm-air curtain hisses at the doors, steam forms where warm air meets −10 °C, grey rubber mats or flattened cardboard lie on wet granite, and grey slush is tracked in.
- **Ventilation kiosks** stand above the line in yards and parks: louvred concrete boxes about 3×3×3 m that breathe warm, damp air and steam in winter, with a low fan drone. This is a strong atmospheric prop.

## 7. Ticket office and fares (2008)
- **КАССА** window: a small glazed hatch with a speaking hole, a recessed coin and note tray, and a cashier in a cardigan behind it. Small signs: «КАССА ЗАКРЫТА» (Ticket office closed) and a price list «СТОИМОСТЬ ПРОЕЗДА» (Fares).
- **Fares from 1 Jan 2008** [V]:

| Ticket | Price |
|---|---|
| 1 trip | **19 р.** |
| 2 trips | 38 р. |
| 5 trips | 84 р. |
| 10 trips | 155 р. |
| 20 trips | 280 р. |
| 60 trips | 580 р. |
| Monthly, up to 70 trips | 600 р. |
| Monthly, unlimited | 860 р. |

  - For comparison: bus or trolleybus 17 р. (25 р. paid to the driver). Metro single fare was 17 р. in 2007 and 22 р. from 2009. [V]
- **Ticket object:** a card-sized (85.6 × 54 mm) thin paper ticket. Design is fictionalised [est.]: white with a blue band, a red М, and «БИЛЕТ НА 1 ПОЕЗДКУ» (ticket for 1 trip). Some magnetic tickets with a brown stripe on the back were still in wallets.

## 8. Turnstiles (2008 mix)
**АКП-73 (1970s)** [V]
- Grey steel cabinets, about 1.0 m high and 1.3 m long, passage about 0.55 m
- The passage stays **open**. Steel flaps **slam shut** at hip height only if you walk through without paying, with a loud "БАМ!" (metal transient plus a pneumatic thump).
- Originally had a magnetic slot in the end face. Many were retrofitted with a contactless **glowing "метка"** (round target) and a small display showing trips left [V]. Colour of the target ring: amber `#FFB400` [est.].
- Indicators: green arrow and red ✕.

**УТ-2000 (from 2000)** [V]
- **Closed** type: opens only after payment. Semi-glass housings, no audible signals.
- A row of 6–10 turnstiles, plus a glass **контролёр** booth at the end with a woman in a dark-blue uniform jacket. Separate exit lanes are marked ВЫХОД.

## 9. Escalators and stairs
**Typical outskirts station:** 2–3 flights of granite stairs, 4–6 m wide, about 4–5 m total rise, with a landing between flights and steel or aluminium handrails.

**If you include an escalator** [K/est.]
- 3 lanes, 30° slope, step width 1.0 m, tread about 0.4 m, total width about 5.5 m
- Rise 4–8 m, so length is about 8–16 m (rise ÷ sin 30°)
- **Speed 0.75 m/s** is the standard, set by Soviet practice [V]. Older units in Moscow are often cited at up to about 0.94 m/s; I could not verify that figure.
- **Handrails:** black rubber `#151515`, about 80 mm wide. They run slightly faster than the steps [V].
- **Steps:** ribbed aluminium `#9EA2A5` with comb plates and yellow edge lines `#E3C21C`. Wooden steps (oak slats `#7A5A3A`) belong to old deep-level escalators in the city centre, not here.
- **Торшеры (floor-lamp balustrade lights):** bronze-tone stalks about 1.2 m above the handrail with frosted cylinder or globe shades, warm `#FFD9A0`, every about 4–5 m along the middle balustrades. They are characteristic of long inclined escalator tunnels. On a short shallow escalator, use ceiling lighting instead, or one or two lamps as a nod.
- **Attendant booth at the bottom:** glazed, about 1.5 × 1.5 × 2.2 m, with a desk lamp, gooseneck microphone, phone, and red emergency-stop buttons. A middle-aged woman attendant in a dark-blue uniform jacket, sometimes reading or knitting.
- **Live attendant announcements** (bored, close-miked, echoey):
  - «Держитесь за поручень» (Hold the handrail)
  - «Не бегите по эскалатору» (Don't run on the escalator)
  - «Не задерживайтесь при сходе с эскалатора» (Don't linger stepping off)
  - «Стойте справа, проходите слева» (Stand on the right, walk on the left)
  - «Не ставьте вещи на ступени» (Don't put bags on the steps)
- **Sound:** a step clack at the comb at about 1.9 Hz at 0.75 m/s (0.4 m treads); motor hum at 50 and 100 Hz; occasional handrail squeak.

## 10. Announcements
**Voice rule:** a **male voice going toward the centre, a female voice going away from it**. On the Ring line, male is clockwise. The female voice was added in 1984 and the rule was proposed by the Society of the Blind. [V]

**In-train automatic announcer, 2008 wording** [V/K]
- Departure: **«Осторожно, двери закрываются. Следующая станция — «Улица Строителей».»** = "Careful, the doors are closing. Next station: Ulitsa Stroiteley."
- Arrival: **«Станция «Улица Строителей».»**, sometimes followed by «Уважаемые пассажиры, при выходе из поезда не забывайте свои вещи» = "Dear passengers, do not forget your belongings when leaving the train."
- Terminus: **«Станция «…». Конечная. Поезд дальше не идёт, просьба освободить вагоны.»** = "…Terminal. This train goes no further, please vacate the cars." This exact phrase was in use in Dec 2007 [V].
- Courtesy: «Уважаемые пассажиры, будьте взаимно вежливы, уступайте места инвалидам, пожилым людям и пассажирам с детьми» = "Be mutually polite, give up seats to the disabled, elderly and passengers with children."
- Security (post-2004 tone, paraphrase): «…о подозрительных предметах сообщайте машинисту» = "report suspicious objects to the driver."

**Live platform PA from the station duty officer** (harsh, reverberant, sometimes clipped)
- «Граждане пассажиры, отойдите от края платформы!» = "Passengers, step back from the platform edge!"
- **«Поезд следует в депо. Посадки нет.»** = "This train is going to the depot. No boarding." Very typical for night.
- «Поезд дальше не идёт, просьба выйти из вагонов» = "The train goes no further, please leave the cars."

**Chime**
- I could not verify a fixed two-tone chime for 2008 Moscow. The 81-717's announcer played no chime before "Осторожно, двери закрываются", and these trains also had **no door-warning beeps**.
- If you want a station PA attention tone, use a soft "динь-дон" before **station PA only** [est.]:
  - E5 659.3 Hz, then C5 523.3 Hz
  - Each tone 0.7 s, exponential decay τ ≈ 0.35 s
  - Partials 1.0 / 2.0 (0.3) / 2.76 (0.12, a bell-like inharmonic)
- PA chain: bandpass 300–3500 Hz, light saturation, RT60 about **2–3 s** on a marble and granite platform.

## 11. Train: 81-717/714 «Номерной»
**Dimensions** [V]
- Length **19.21 m** (about 77 voxels), width **2.67 m** (about 11), height **3.65 m** (about 15)
- **4 doors per side**, door opening about 1.25–1.38 m [est., 5 voxels]
- Wheel diameter 780 mm
- Max speed 90 km/h, service speed 80 km/h; acceleration 1.2 m/s², braking 1.1 m/s²
- Traction motors **4 × ДК-117 (110–114 kW)**, rheostatic braking plus Westinghouse air brakes
- Seats: 40–42 in the head car (81-717), 44–48 in the intermediate car (81-714). Intermediate cars have no cab. [V]
- Bogie wheelbase about 2.1 m, bogie centres about 12.6 m apart [K]

**Nickname:** «номерной» ("numbered") because the type got no letter designation [V]. Usually 8-car trains on the outskirts lines in 2008.

**Livery:** classic **blue and light blue (сине-голубой)** [V]. Use light blue upper body and window band `#7EA6CF`, darker blue lower body and skirt `#2C4B8E`, grey roof `#8C9093` darkened by brake dust, near-black underframe `#262626` [K/est.].

**Head car front** [V/K]
- Large central windshield with a driver seated in the **centre**, two small side windows
- Small route-number lightbox in the windshield
- 4-digit car number on the front
- **Headlights:** four under the windshield on later 81-717.5М cars, which use light lilac-blue lamps; older cars use **amber-yellow incandescent** lamps `#FFC46A` [V]. Two red marker lights on the rear car `#E0201A`.
- Scharfenberg-type coupler [K]

**Interior** [V/K]
- Fluorescent lamps in **two rows** of plastic diffusers along the ceiling, cool `#EEF3EE`
- Walls: decorative fire-resistant plastic, light beige with a fine pattern `#E2D8C3` [V/est.]
- Seats: originally long benches in hard **brown leatherette** `#6B4230`. From the late 1990s, anti-vandal plastic seats with leatherette inserts, grey-blue `#7A8796` with dark-blue inserts `#2F3F68` [V/est.].
- Steel grab poles and overhead rails `#B9BDC1`, brown-grey linoleum floor `#5A4B3F`
- Small sliding top vents (форточки) in the windows
- Lights briefly dim or flicker at gaps in the third rail [K]

**Sound design** [K/est.]
- **Departure:**
  - The doors close with a short air hiss and a hard double-leaf **slam** (transient at 80–150 Hz plus a metallic rattle).
  - Then a run of **contactor and rheostat-controller clicks** under the floor, about 12–18 clicks over the first about 10 s.
  - A traction **whine** rises with speed. Gear-mesh estimate: about **9 Hz per km/h**, so about 540 Hz at 60 km/h and about 725 Hz at 80 km/h (gear ratio about 5.33, 15-tooth pinion, both estimated). Add a 2nd harmonic at −12 dB and slight volume steps at each controller notch. The stepped, slightly "sour" quality of that whine is what makes the 81-717 recognisable.
- **Braking:** the whine falls from about 600 to 100 Hz over 8–10 s, followed by a narrowband brake squeal at 2–4 kHz below about 8 km/h and a final **"пшш"** (noise burst, high-pass 2 kHz, 0.7 s).
- **Compressor** (motor-compressor, standing at the platform): a low thumping "тук-тук-тук" at about 12–16 Hz pulse rate over a 50–100 Hz drone, running 20–40 s, ending in a hiss.
- **Wheels:** a double "та-дах" per bogie at insulated rail joints.
- **Arrival from the tunnel:** brown noise building over about 10 s through a low-pass opening from 300 to 1500 Hz, plus a **piston-wind** gust that moves litter and hair. Train headlights show in the tunnel before the sound peaks.
- **In-car noise in the tunnel:** loud, about 85–95 dB in reality. Mix it as a dominant rumble.
- **Horn:** a short, harsh low horn of about 350 Hz, used when someone is near the edge.

## 12. Operations at night
- Entrances close at **01:00**; last trains run until about 01:00–01:40; stations open at **05:30** [V]
- After 23:00, intervals are **up to about 8 min** [V]. Use 5–8 min late in the evening and 8–10 min near closing [est.].
- After about 00:30, half-empty trains run to the depot and terminate with «Посадки нет».
- Overnight: floor-scrubbing machines hum and track crews walk the tunnels with lamps.
- Station staff: the station duty officer in a **red cap** [K], cleaners in blue smocks with mop carts, and a pair of милиционеры checking documents near the turnstiles.

## 13. Tunnels
- **Shallow lines:** rectangular cut-and-cover reinforced-concrete boxes, flat ceiling, about 4.5 m high, single-track width about 5 m [est.]
- **Bored tunnels:** circular, inner diameter about 5.1–5.5 m, cast-iron or concrete lining rings 0.75–1.0 m wide with visible ribs and bolts [K]
- **Track:** concrete bed with embedded wooden half-sleepers and a central drainage channel; third rail on one side under a cover
- **Cables:** 15–40 black cables `#1C1C1C` on steel brackets in 2–4 tiers at 1.0–2.5 m height, sagging slightly between brackets
- **Walls:** sooty grey `#4A4743` with rust streaks `#7A4A2A` and whitewashed patches
- **Lamps:** dim warm incandescent bulkhead fittings `#FFC87A` every about 15–25 m
- **Signals:** red, yellow, green and moon-white heads near stations; white kilometre or picket plates with black digits every 100 m
- **Smell cue:** creosote and brake dust
