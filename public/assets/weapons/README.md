# Weapons (firearms)

30 first-person/third-person firearm models for the PUBG-style roster, one GLB per weapon id
(`ar_m416.glb`, `sr_kar98k.glb`, ...). Per-weapon data (length, bounds, triangle count, node list, reference
image) is in `manifest.json`. A contact sheet of every weapon (side and 3/4 views) is at
`tools/assets/weapons/preview/contact_sheet.jpg`. To re-render it, run
`node tools/assets/weapons/preview/contact_sheet.mjs out.png [--markers 1]`.

Totals: 30 files, 12.5 MB (0.30-0.60 MB each), 3.5k-13.6k triangles each, 1 material and 3 x 1024 px JPEG
textures per weapon.

The `melee_*` and `throw_*` files in this folder belong to the gear pipeline (`tools/assets/gear`), not to this
document. `make_manifest.mjs` keeps any manifest entries it does not own.

## Source and license

Every model in this folder is **authored for this project** (license CC0, no attribution required). They are
generated by scripted Blender modelling in `tools/assets/weapons/`:

- `ref/<id>.json` holds a millimetre side silhouette traced automatically (`trace.py`) from a Wikimedia Commons
  side-view photo of the real weapon and scaled to the real overall length. The photos are not stored in the
  repo, only the traced outlines. `ref_sources.json` lists the photo used for each weapon. 21 guns are traced.
  Five (SKS, Mini 14, SLR, M16A4, Tommy) use hand-drawn outlines read off the photo grid. Four (S686,
  sawed-off, S1897, DP-28) are authored from published dimensions.
- `guns/<id>.py` splits the silhouette into parts (receiver, stock, grip, magazine, handguard and so on),
  extrudes each part at its real thickness with rounded bevels, and adds turned parts (barrels, muzzle devices,
  gas tubes), Picatinny rails with real slot pitch, iron sights, triggers, pins and controls.
- `wkit.py` has the kit: profile extrusion with eroded round bevels, lathe, rails, procedural PBR materials
  (parkerized steel, bluing, anodized aluminium, polymer with stipple, three walnut/beech wood tones with grain,
  rubber, bakelite, painted steel, brass), weighted normals, UV packing, and a Cycles bake. The bake produces edge wear, AO,
  cavity grime, the bevel normal and the micro-surface normal into one 1024 px texture set per weapon.

Why authored? I searched Poly Haven, OpenGameArt, itch.io CC0 packs, Quaternius, Kenney and three.js/Godot
sample repos. The free models that exist are either low-poly or stylized (Quaternius, Styloo, the flat CC0
packs), or cover only 3-6 roster guns at inconsistent quality and scale (the 3dmodelscc0 pack: AK-47, M4A1,
Makarov). Poly Haven's two firearms, a scoped Mosin-style bolt-action rifle and a Cold War service pistol, are
excellent but do not match roster guns. None had the separate magazine and bolt meshes the code needs. Authoring all 30 with one kit gives a
consistent scale, materials, node layout and texel density.

## Conventions (all files)

- glTF axes: **+Y up, barrel along +Z, meters, real-world length**. The gun's right-hand (ejection) side is **-X**.
- The root node is named after the weapon id. **Its origin is the firing-hand grip**: the middle of the pistol
  grip, or the stock wrist on the DP-28, Kar98k, SKS, Mini 14, Win94, S1897, S686 and M24.
- Child nodes:

| node | type | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `body` | mesh | everything static |
| `mag` | pivot empty → `mag_mesh` | magazine. Pivot at the top of the mag well. It is the pan on the DP-28, the cylinder on the R1895 (pivot on the cylinder axis), the shells in the chambers on the S686 and sawed-off, the 200-rd box on the M249, the loading gate on the Win94, the shell at the loading port on the S1897, and the floor plate on the Kar98k |
| `bolt` / `charging_handle` / `slide` | pivot empty → `<name>_mesh` | the part that cycles. Which one a gun has is given by `cycle_node` in the manifest |
| `muzzle` | empty | bore axis at the muzzle face (muzzle flash / projectile spawn) |
| `grip_support` | empty | support-hand position (handguard, foregrip, pump or fore-end; pistols: support hand wrapped under the grip) |
| `sight_eye` | empty | eye point for iron-sight ADS, on the line through the rear and front sight tops. Long guns: cheek-weld distance. Pistols: 260 mm behind the rear sight, arms extended |
| `rail_top` | empty | where an optic's mount base sits (top of the Picatinny rail, receiver cover or claw-mount base). Absent on guns that take no optic: Uzi, Tommy, S1897, S686, sawed-off, DP-28. On the P92, P1911 and P18C it is a child of `slide` |

- Animate the **pivot empty** (`mag`, `bolt`, ...), not the `*_mesh` child. Meshopt quantization rewrites
  mesh-node transforms, so mesh-node origins are not meaningful.
- Suggested motions in the gun's local frame:
  - Charging handles and bolt carriers: translate along -Z by the real stroke (AR/SCAR/AUG ~75-90 mm, AK
    family ~110 mm, SMGs ~60-70 mm, M249 ~110 mm, pistols' `slide` ~45-60 mm).
  - Bolt actions (Kar98k, M24, AWM): rotate `bolt` about +Z by about 60-90°, then translate -Z by about 90 mm.
    The pivot is on the bore axis.
  - Win94: rotate `bolt` (the lever) about X by about 45-55°. The lever swings down and forward. S1897: translate
    `bolt` (the slide block) along -Z by about 70 mm and move the pump with your own code if needed. The pump is
    part of `body`.
  - Break actions (S686, sawed-off): `bolt` = barrels plus fore-end, pivoting on the hinge pin. Rotate about X
    so the muzzle drops about 30°.
  - R1895: `bolt` = hammer (rotate about X), `mag` = cylinder (spin about Z, 360/7° per shot).
  - Magazines: translate `mag` along local -Y, or along the mag well axis for the curved AK and Beryl mags,
    which drop slightly forward. Rotate the DP-28 pan up about Z.
- Materials: one PBR material per weapon with embedded JPEG baseColor, normal and ORM (R = AO, G = roughness,
  B = metalness, used for both `occlusionTexture` and `metallicRoughnessTexture`), at 1024 px.
- Compression: `EXT_meshopt_compression` + `KHR_mesh_quantization`. Load with `GLTFLoader.setMeshoptDecoder`.
  No Draco or KTX2.

## Quality notes

- Silhouettes are traced from real photos at real scale, so proportions, grip angles, magazine curves and
  stock shapes match the real weapons. Every part has rounded bevels and weighted normals, so nothing looks
  faceted.
- Textures are procedural: edge wear on convex edges (from a per-part baked thickness mask), cavity grime and
  AO, roughness variation, stipple on polymer, real wood grain and figure. There are **no stamped markings,
  serial numbers or text**.
- Rails are separate geometry with real teeth. Holes are fake: dark inset discs on the DP-28 shroud and
  dark slots on muzzle devices and handguards. Real geometric cutouts appear only in outlines (trigger guards,
  sight apertures, the Vector frame).
- Bipods on the M249 and DP-28 are modelled folded along the barrel.
- Optics are not included. Sniper rifles carry an optics rail (M24, AWM) or `rail_top` on the receiver
  (Kar98k, for a claw mount).

## Known gaps and recommendations

- Weapon-specific approximations: the M16A4 is an A2 photo outline converted to a flat-top with BUIS. The SKS
  has the detachable box magazine of the game version. The S12K uses the Saiga-12K stock and magazine
  silhouette. Pistol slide details (Beretta open-top cut, 1911 grip safety) are simplified.
- The textures look like well-kept service weapons. For extra realism, add a world-space dirt/scratch detail
  map in the shader at close range (first person).
- No LODs are included. At 3.5k-13.6k triangles one LOD is fine for first person. For third-person/world pickups
  at distance, `gltf-transform simplify` to about 30% works well because there are no thin shells to collapse.
- To change a gun, edit `tools/assets/weapons/guns/<id>.py` and run
  `BLENDER=<blender> tools/assets/weapons/build_all.sh <work_dir> <id>`. That rebuilds, bakes, optimizes,
  copies into this folder and regenerates `manifest.json`.
